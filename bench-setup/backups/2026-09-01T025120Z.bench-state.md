## How it works

Every fenced ```setup block in this document is concatenated, **in document order**, to form
`tb470.setup`. Prose outside those fences (this section, the diagram, the evidence log) is for
readers here and never reaches the testbox. Prose that testbox users must see goes *inside* a
fence as a `###` comment.

| command | what it does |
| --- | --- |
| `./bench_setup.py render` | render to stdout, touch nothing |
| `./bench_setup.py check` | render, compare against the live file, report drift, exit 1 if it differs |
| `./bench_setup.py apply` | snapshot the live file into `backups/`, then write it in place |

`apply` refuses to run if the live file has drifted from the last snapshot *and* from the
render — that means someone hand-edited the box, and overwriting would lose their work.

**No more `.bak` files beside the live file.** History lives in `backups/` here, named
`tb470.setup.<UTC ISO timestamp>`. The two `tb470.setup.bak-2026-08-*` files in there keep
their original names on purpose: they are the last of the old scheme, lifted off the box
before it was cleaned up, and the name records where they came from.

## Evidence, and what is inferred rather than measured

Raw capture: `~/old test runs/IE520/stack-tests/bench-probe-2026-09-01/capture.json`
(`ask-ck/test-composer/bench_probe.py`, three devices, nine commands each).

**Measured.** The LAG exists and lands on the AR4050S, proven from both ends: the stack reports
partner LAG `0x8000,00-00-cd-40-03-94,0x0002` and the 4050 reports `0x8000,00-00-cd-37-0d-6f,0x0001`.
`tb eth3 <-> swi_c port1.0.1` is pinned by the 4050 holding `00f0.4d00.7718` on that port with a
matching ARP entry for `10.38.215.65`.

**Inferred — do not treat as measured.**

- *Which LAG leg pairs with which.* LACP names the partner **system**, not the partner **port**.
  `swi_b port1.0.1 <-> swi_c port1.0.4` carries over from a MAC cross-match taken before the
  aggregation formed; the other leg follows by elimination. Proving it means shutting one 4050
  member and watching which stack port drops — mutating, needs sign-off, and it degrades the LAG
  while it runs.
- *`swi_d port1.0.2 <-> swi_c port1.0.2`.* By elimination: the 4050 has exactly four ports up and
  three are accounted for; the x230 has exactly one port connected and learns the 4050 base MAC
  on its `sa1`.

**The trap that bit us on 2026-09-01.** The 4050 runs `lacp global-passive-mode enable`. It never
*initiates* an aggregation but will passively bond anything presenting LACP — so `po2` appeared by
itself and is **not in the 4050's running-config**; only `show etherchannel detail` reveals it. The
corollary: two links to the 4050 that are *not* aggregated on the other end stay independent and
**loop**. Measured that day as the 4050's own MAC thrashing between IE520 `port1.0.1` and
`port2.0.9`, costing ~30% of a `show tech-support` collection.
