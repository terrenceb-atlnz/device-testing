| T44987 | ACL: Hardware IP 3000 range, applied to ports | [T44987/T44987.log](T44987/T44987.log) | **PASS**. Numbered hardware IP ACLs (3000–3699) bind to a port with `access-group` and filter per protocol. All five forms were tested in isolation against a non-matching control source: `icmp`, `ip` and `proto 1` each deny the matching flow while `tcp` correctly does not match ICMP — the negative control that gives the others meaning. A `permit` rule was proven by its own counter advancing rather than by the implicit permit. Five lists bound to one port simultaneously were accepted, consuming exactly one classifier entry each. The configuration, the binding and the enforcement all survived a reload. |
| T44988 | ACL: Hardware MAC 4000 range, applied to ports | [T44988/T44988.log](T44988/T44988.log) | **PASS**. Numbered hardware MAC ACLs (4000–4699) bind to a port and filter on source and destination MAC. Exact masks denied the matching flow, a MAC present nowhere on the bench matched nothing, `deny any any` denied everything, and the VLAN qualifier was proven selective by a matching/non-matching pair on otherwise identical rules. The mask was proven to be a wildcard from two independent directions — a spoofed source MAC matching an all-ones rule that does not name it, and an absent MAC with an all-ones mask matching ordinary traffic. `copy-to-cpu` was proven on both halves of its definition. |
| T44986 | deny/permit statements for standard access list | [T44986/T44986.log](T44986/T44986.log) | **PASS**. All five behaviours — permit, deny, implicit deny, wildcard mask selection, and first-match top-to-bottom evaluation — were demonstrated on `vty access-class` against SSH from two client addresses, each row paired with its control. A standard 1–99 list cannot be bound to an interface on this platform and the Cisco-style line-mode `access-class` does not exist; the binding point is `vty access-class` in global configuration. Recorded as a finding: `access-group <1-99>` reports `% Access-list does not exist` for a list that demonstrably does exist. |
